# Actionable Feedback and Revision Performance in College Writing

## Abstract

We report a randomized controlled trial (N = 120) comparing the effects of generic praise, criticism-only comments, and "actionable feedback" on undergraduate revision performance. Actionable feedback was defined as comments that named the gap between current performance and a stated success criterion and suggested a concrete next step. Students in the actionable-feedback condition revised 34% more target sentences correctly than those in the generic-praise condition (d = 0.62, 95% CI [0.18, 1.06]) and 21% more than those in the criticism-only condition (d = 0.41, 95% CI [0.02, 0.80]). The effect persisted in a follow-up writing task two weeks later.

## Method

Participants were first-year students in a required writing course at a large public university. We randomly assigned 40 students to each of three feedback conditions. All students submitted a 500-word argumentative essay and received comments on three target sentences. Raters blind to condition coded revisions as correct, partial, or incorrect. Inter-rater reliability was κ = 0.84.

## Results and Limitation

The actionable-feedback advantage was largest for students who had not previously taken a writing-intensive course. A limitation is that the sample was drawn from a single institution and focused on short revisions; whether the effect generalizes to longer compositions or other disciplines remains open.
