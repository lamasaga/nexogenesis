### 核心表达

　　The Canadian banking system is organized around **chartered banks** that function as nationwide **branch-banking** institutions, with the head office supervising branches that conduct all actual deposit and lending business, a structure that the author traces to pre-Civil War American banking precedents.

### 依据与细节

- As of the writing, twenty-nine "chartered banks" conducted all banking business in Canada, maintaining approximately 2,200 branches (rising to about 3,200 branches among twenty-two banks by the close of 1915).
- The law places no restriction on the number or location of branches, so Canadian banks operate foreign branches as well as domestic ones.
- The head office neither takes deposits nor lends money; all banking business is done by branches, each enjoying considerable independence but subject to head-office supervision and control.
- The system evolved gradually from commercial and financial needs rather than being created by lawyers or statesmen to meet a government fiscal need.
- The Bank Act prohibits any institution other than a chartered bank from using the word "bank."
- Chartered banks buy and sell commercial paper, discount customer notes, lend on stocks and bonds, make advances to farmers, and sometimes aid railroad and industrial financing.

### 限制与边界

- The description reflects conditions up to 1915 and may not describe post-war or modern Canadian banking law.
- The text focuses on the Dominion of Canada and does not systematically compare the contemporary United States national-banking structure beyond selected historical antecedents.
- 原文未提及：specific reserve requirements for deposits, or the precise post-1908 amendments beyond the emergency-circulation provision.

### 原文摘录

> "A chartered bank in Canada is a bank of branches, not a bank with branches."
> "The Canadian system is a product of evolution. It has taken its present form because of the commercial and financial needs of the Canadian people."
> "To a Canadian the word 'bank' means one of the twenty-odd 'chartered banks,' for the law prohibits the use of the word 'bank' by any other institution."
