### 核心表达

　　Canadian chartered banks may issue circulating notes up to the amount of their unimpaired paid-up capital without depositing security with the government, and the absence of such security does not undermine public confidence because redemption is tested daily at clearing houses.

### 依据与细节

- A bank may issue notes in denominations of $5 and multiples thereof up to the amount of its unimpaired paid-up capital.
- The July 20, 1908 amendment permits an emergency circulation during the crop-moving season (October 1 to January 31), raising the legal maximum to paid-up capital plus 15 per cent. of combined paid-up capital and surplus or rest fund.
- Emergency notes are taxed at a rate not exceeding 5 per cent. per annum, fixed by the governor in council; no tax is paid if circulation remains within paid-up capital.
- The law does not require a cash reserve against notes or deposits, does not make notes legal tender, does not require banks to accept other banks' notes, and does not guarantee redemption.
- Nobody since 1890 had lost a dollar through a bank's failure to redeem its notes.

### 限制与边界

- The argument assumes a branch-banking structure with redemption centres across the Dominion; it may not transfer to a unit-banking system.
- Public confidence is described as habitual rather than analytically grounded in the 5 per cent. fund.
- 原文未提及：the exact statutory text of the Bank Act, or how the 1908 amendment was administered in practice beyond the tax-rate ceiling.

### 原文摘录

> "The law is silent on several subjects that seem of great importance to most bankers in the United States. For instance, it does not require that the banks shall deposit with a government official, or in any way set aside any kind of security for the protection of the note holder."
> "Nevertheless the notes of the Canadian banks are everywhere acceptable at par, the people apparently not being at all concerned about their 'goodness.'"
> "But the abolishment of this redemption system would at once give Canada a new banking system."
