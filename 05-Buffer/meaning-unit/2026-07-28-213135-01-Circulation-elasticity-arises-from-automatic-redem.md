### 核心表达

　　The Canadian note supply is self-regulating: daily redemption at multiple centres and the absence of competing government paper currency in denominations of $5 and above cause the public, not the banks, to determine the actual volume of bank notes in circulation.

### 依据与细节

- Two facts make over-issue impossible: (1) every bank must redeem its notes on demand at seven commercial centres across the Dominion, and (2) the monetary circulation, excluding $1 and $2 bills and change, consists entirely of bank notes.
- Country merchants deposit surplus notes with their local branch; the branch sorts out other banks' notes and sends them for redemption or offsets them in the local clearing house.
- Branches are not obliged to redeem their parent bank's notes but must accept them at par for all dues, so each bank pays out its own notes while redeeming others'.
- Because Canada lacks government notes in denominations of $5 and above, new bank notes cannot simply displace government paper and expand into a larger total circulation; if business slackens, notes flow back into banks as deposits.
- The author contrasts this with a hypothetical United States-like situation where government notes in $5, $10, and $20 denominations would allow bank notes to replace them, expanding deposits and loans and producing inflation.

### 限制与边界

- The self-regulation argument assumes convertibility into gold or its equivalent is always credible.
- The analysis compares a real Canadian system with a hypothetical Americanized Canada, not with the actual contemporary United States.
- 原文未提及：the seasonal pattern of note demand outside the crop-moving season, or data on actual month-to-month fluctuations.

### 原文摘录

> "It is the people, not the banks, who determine how much the circulation of the banks shall be."
> "If Canada, like the United States, had in circulation a lot of government notes in denominations of $5, $10, $20, the Canadian banks would be able to increase their issues of bank notes almost without limit."
> "Hence, if at any time business relaxes and the need for money among the people grows less, an increasing tide of bank notes flows into the banks."
