### 核心表达

　　American manufacturers often treat export sales to foreign customers as routine transactions, but the credit element is both real and underappreciated: the foreign customer may change his mind, refuse the goods, or accept the goods and draft and still fail to pay. A draft on a foreign customer, especially when accepted, is not merely a payment device but a legally enforceable acknowledgment of indebtedness.

### 依据与细节

　　The credit risks catalogued include:
- The customer changes his mind after the exporter has purchased goods from the manufacturer;
- The customer refuses to accept the goods;
- The customer accepts the goods and the draft but fails to pay at maturity.

　　Two documentary arrangements are distinguished:
- **Documents against payment (d/p):** the customer pays the value of the draft in cash before receiving documents/goods.
- **Documents against acceptance (d/a):** the customer accepts the draft for future payment (one to three months) and receives documents/goods afterward; this is described as "the more customary course."

　　Even under d/p, payment may be postponed. In the Far East, banks maintain "godowns" (warehouses) where goods are held until the customer can pay; goods may be released against a **trust receipt** for warehousing purposes. The author notes that an accepted draft is valuable judicially because it forms an acknowledgment of indebtedness, making collection simpler than open-account collection. When unpaid, an accepted draft is **protested**, and debtors can be proceeded against directly.

### 限制与边界

　　原文未提及： comparative default rates between d/p and d/a; the cost of protest proceedings across jurisdictions; whether trust receipts were enforceable in all trading countries; the role of export credit insurance.

### 原文摘录

> "Many American manufacturers do not realize the essential 'credit' element of transactions on the basis of drafts drawn on foreign customers."
> "One important feature of 'acceptance' of the draft by the customer is the fact that it forms an acknowledgment of indebtedness, which it is then unnecessary to prove item by item in case of litigation."
> "To neglect the payment of an accepted draft is regarded in the trade and by banks as so serious a matter that the drawee would lose caste with the banks."
