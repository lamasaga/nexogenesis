### 核心表达

　　Canadian banking law treats merchandise and business assets broadly as collateral for bank advances, giving chartered banks a status close to silent partners in wholesale and manufacturing enterprises.

### 依据与细节

　　- Much commercial paper is secured practically by title to goods in warehouses, factories, and wholesale stores, which is considered more saleable than stock-market collateral.
　　- The Bank Act assumes that if a bank advances capital for a business, it should have a claim upon all assets and upon all goods as they come and go in trade.
　　- A merchant's changing stock belongs to the bank in case of default; similarly, a manufacturer's raw materials and finished products become bank property if the note is unpaid.
　　- This legal position leads bankers to follow commodity prices, tariffs, lumber markets, metal prices, and foreign-market developments closely.

### 原文摘录

> So far as would seem possible the Canadian Bank Act makes merchandise of all kinds a sort of collateral security for bank advances. It assumes that if a bank advances capital for the conduct of a business it should have a claim upon all the assets of the business and upon all goods as they come and go in the course of trade.
> The law practically makes every bank a silent partner in many wholesale and manufacturing businesses and gives it many rights which no ordinary silent partner can acquire.
> He is in a sense the owner of merchandise of all kinds, and both trade and financial news has equal significance to him.
