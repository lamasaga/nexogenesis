### 核心表达

　　Under Canadian branch banking, surplus funds accumulated in slow-growing Eastern branches are transferred westward to finance loans in rapidly developing regions, producing a more uniform interest rate across the Dominion but also diverting local capital from the East.

### 依据与细节

　　- Eastern branches collect heavy deposits and are creditors of the head office; funds are forwarded to Western branches whose loans greatly exceed deposits.
　　- Between 1900 and 1909, total Canadian bank clearings increased 227 per cent; Halifax gained only 23 per cent, St. John 90 per cent, Quebec 68 per cent, while Toronto rose 179 per cent, Winnipeg 600 per cent, and Vancouver 524 per cent.
　　- Bankers are said to ask three questions: Where is idle money accumulating? How can it be drawn into the bank? In what parts of the Dominion is money most needed?
　　- This capital movement tends toward uniformity of interest rates; a merchant or manufacturer in Manitoba can obtain money as easily as one in Toronto or Montreal.

### 原文摘录

> It is generally known that the Eastern branches get heavy deposits and are creditors of the head office, and that the funds they collect are forwarded to the Western branches, whose loans greatly exceed deposits.
> Between 1900 and 1909 the total of Canada's bank clearings increased 227 per cent., but Halifax gained only 23 per cent., St. John only 90 per cent., and Quebec only 68 per cent. On the other hand, Toronto's clearings increased 179 per cent., Winnipeg's 600 per cent., and Vancouver's 524 per cent.
> Whatever may be said against a system of branch banks, there can be no question that it does bring about a more even distribution of capital in a country than is possible under a system of independent local banks.
