### 核心表达

　　Canadian chartered banks do not distinguish sharply between time or savings deposits and demand deposits in practice; both are regarded as essentially the same form of liability and are payable on demand, even though the law may permit notice requirements for some classes.

### 依据与细节

　　- Canadian banks regard time and savings deposits as essentially the same liability because a large proportion of such funds is likely to be needed in business at any time.
　　- Practically all deposit liabilities are payable on demand, although payment on about two-thirds of them cannot legally be demanded until after notice.
　　- Custom has made it imperative that a bank pay any depositor on demand; refusal would be taken by the public as an acknowledgment of weakness.
　　- Bankers feel that 3 per cent is too high to pay depositors, but the rate is sustained by tacit agreement; no single bank can lower it without losing business, and any bank offering 3-1/2 or 4 per cent is viewed with grave disapproval by competitors.

### 原文摘录

> On account of the fact that the time or savings bank deposits contain such a large proportion of money likely to be needed in business at any time, the banks regard both classes of deposit as being essentially the same form of liability.
> Practically all the deposit liabilities of a Canadian bank are payable on demand, although payment on two-thirds of them at the present time can not legally be demanded until after notice.
> For any bank to refuse to let a depositor have his money when he calls for it would be regarded by the public as an acknowledgment of weakness.
