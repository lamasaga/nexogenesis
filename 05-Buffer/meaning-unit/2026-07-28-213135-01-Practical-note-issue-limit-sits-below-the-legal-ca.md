### 核心表达

　　Although Canadian law caps note issue at paid-up capital, the effective operational limit is roughly 10 per cent. below that ceiling because bank managers begin to restrict note outflows once circulation reaches about 90 per cent. of capital.

### 依据与细节

- The legal limit is fixed at the amount of a bank's paid-up capital; the 1908 amendment adds an emergency tier taxed up to 5 per cent. per annum.
- The author argues there is no scientific necessity for a statutory limit because even a monopoly issuer such as the Bank of France cannot expand circulation beyond public needs.
- The limit is justified indirectly as a way to compel banks to increase capitalization pari passu with business growth, since bank strength varies with capital.
- A manager with $1,000,000 paid-up capital becomes cautious when circulation reaches $900,000 and warns branches against large note disbursements or transactions likely to create unusual currency demand.
- In 1907, some banks reached the legal limit; had the market rate not justified issuing taxed emergency notes, circulation would have lost elasticity, forcing banks to pay out gold and Dominion notes and deplete reserves.

### 限制与边界

- The 10 per cent. margin is presented as a rule of thumb based on managerial behavior, not a statutory or empirical average.
- The 1907 episode is described briefly; no comprehensive data on all banks' circulation ratios are provided.
- 原文未提及：how often the emergency-circulation privilege was used, or the precise tax rate actually charged.

### 原文摘录

> "It should be noted that the practical limit of note issue is about 10 per cent. below the legal limit."
> "His office may be in Montreal and his bank may have branches in the far East and in the far West and in the mining wilderness of the North."
> "If all the banks in Canada should issue notes up to the limit, as some of them did during the exciting months of 1907, and if the current rate of interest did not warrant the issue of the taxed notes provided for by the amendment of 1908, the note circulation would immediately lose its elasticity."
