### 核心表达

　　After the National Bank Act and the 10 per cent tax on state-bank notes made state bank notes practically obsolete, the rationale for state banking regulation gradually shifted from securing the safety of bank notes to protecting depositors, mirroring the regulatory philosophy of the national-bank act.

### 依据与细节

　　- In 1860 there were 1,562 state banks in the United States; by 1868 the number had fallen to 247, attributed to the repressive influence of the national-bank act hastened by the 10 per cent tax on state-bank notes.
　　- The decline caused state banking legislation to cease; old laws regulating banks of issue were swept away by code revisions or remained obsolete.
　　- State banks began to increase again around 1870. As late as 1892, Stimson's digest of state statute law stated that state banking laws had become "obsolete in use and form" because they were superseded by national banking laws.
　　- The antebellum state-bank regulations were intended to secure the safety of the bank note; depositor protection was purely incidental.
　　- The view that note-issuing banks alone required governmental regulation persisted for some time after the National Bank Act, because national banks held a monopoly of bank-note issue.
　　- As the importance of note issue as a banking function decreased, banking regulation began to be considered desirable as a protection to depositors.

### 限制与边界

　　- The text does not quantify the relative importance of note safety versus depositor protection over time, nor does it evaluate how effective the new depositor-focused regulations were.
　　- The source distinguishes state banks from mutual savings banks, stock savings banks, trust companies, and private banks; the regulatory shift described applies specifically to state banks of discount and deposit.

### 原文摘录

> The antebellum state-bank regulations were intended to secure the safety of the bank note. Although the depositor was protected by many of the regulations, this protection was purely incidental.
> Since the national banks had a monopoly of the issue of bank notes, the regulation of state banks was considered needless. As the importance of note issue as a banking function decreased, banking regulation, as seen in the national-bank act, began to be considered desirable as a protection to depositors.
