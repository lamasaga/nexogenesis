### 核心表达

　　After the practical prohibition of state-bank note issue in 1866 reduced state banks to ordinary corporate form, nearly all important banking States reimposed additional statutory liability on stockholders of state banks and trust companies, but this liability proved of little service to creditors because enforcement remained ineffective.

### 依据与细节

　　- With the practical prohibition of state bank notes in 1866 and the consequent decrease in the number of state banks, stockholder liability in state banks became the same as that of ordinary business corporations, except where state constitutions imposed additional liability.
　　- Since 1880, provisions imposing additional liability on stockholders of banking corporations were placed in the banking and trust-company laws of nearly all States in which state banks or trust companies had assumed great importance.
　　- In the larger number of States and Territories the liability was proportionate: stockholders were responsible "equally and ratably and not one for another."
　　- The imposition of statutory liability "has not proved of great service as a protection to bank creditors against loss."
　　- "As yet little has been accomplished in the way of making the enforcement of the liability effective."

### 限制与边界

　　- The source does not explain the legal or practical obstacles to enforcement, nor does it compare states with effective versus ineffective enforcement.
　　- No quantitative data is provided on recoveries from stockholders under these statutes.
