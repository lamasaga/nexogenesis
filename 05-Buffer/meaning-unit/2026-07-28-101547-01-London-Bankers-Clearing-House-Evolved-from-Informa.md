### 核心表达

The London Bankers' Clearing House developed gradually from informal exchanges among bank clerks sent on daily "walks" to collect cash or notes for customers' drafts. The need to reduce robbery risk and duplicated labour among some fifty private banking firms led first to rotary meetings and then, around 1770, to regular gatherings at a public house near Lombard Street. Joint-stock banks were admitted only after 1854, subject to a twelve-month operating-history requirement.

### 依据与细节

- Before formal organization, each bank sent clerks on "walks" to other bankers to obtain cash or notes for drafts.
- About **1770**, walk clerks from city and West End banks began meeting after lunch at the **Five Bells in Dove Court, Lombard Street**, close to St. Mary Woolnoth Church and near the present clearing-house site.
- Exchange of checks took place in the public room or, by tradition, on the posts in the court outside; balances were settled in notes and cash.
- In **May 1854** the clearing house closed for alterations and temporary business moved to the Hall of Commerce.
- On **6 June 1854** applications were received from five joint-stock banks: **London and Westminster**, **London Joint Stock**, **Union Bank of London**, **Commercial Bank of London**, and **London and County Bank**.
- At that time there were **25 private banks** already in the clearing house.
- Admission was resolved subject to an annual sum fixed by the committee to reimburse outlay, and later applications were refused unless the bank had been in operation for at least **twelve months**.

### 限制与边界

The exact origin is described as probably undeterminable; some details rest on tradition rather than documentary evidence. The text does not describe the post-1854 governance rules in detail, nor does it compare London's later structure with the American systems discussed earlier.
