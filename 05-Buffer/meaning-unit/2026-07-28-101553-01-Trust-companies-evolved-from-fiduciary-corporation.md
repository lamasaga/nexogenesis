### 核心表达

　　The trust company began as a corporation specialising in administering trusts and ancillary insurance services, but economic pressures drove two convergent tendencies: the elimination of fidelity/title insurance functions and the expansion of banking powers, producing a standardised institution that is essentially a bank with trust powers.

### 依据与细节

　　- Early trust-company laws showed wide disagreement about field of operation; the only common point was that a corporation could administer trusts more advantageously and safely than an individual.
　　- Additional early powers included life insurance, annuities, fidelity insurance, title insurance, and safe-deposit business.
　　- Fidelity insurance was eliminated because the business requires numerous and widely distributed risks best met by specialised national companies, not because of adverse legislation.
　　- Title insurance was eliminated because each city tends to support a single company with an expensive plant devoted to that one business.
　　- Banking-power expansion arose from the difficulty of distinguishing trust-company powers from the banking powers of state and national banks; courts often held trust companies empowered to do banking, or companies altered methods to fit conferred powers.
　　- Missouri example (since 1885): empowered to "receive money in trust and to accumulate the same at such rate as may be obtained or agreed upon or to allow such interest thereon as may be agreed"; the supreme court held this permitted interest-bearing demand deposits payable on check, with a nominal interest rate.
　　- Pennsylvania example: 1874 act provided for title-insurance companies; 1881 amendment gave trust and fidelity-insurance powers to companies with capital of at least $250,000 but expressly prohibited banking; 1885 allowed deposits for safekeeping; 1895 allowed deposits of money and personal property and issuance of obligations; 1900 circuit court decided demand and time deposits were legal; even now Pennsylvania trust companies cannot discount commercial paper but may loan on it as collateral or purchase it.
　　- Restrictive states included Iowa, Michigan, Nebraska, and Wisconsin; Nebraska prohibited banking, Iowa allowed only time deposits and drafts on depositories, Michigan forbade "a general banking business," and Minnesota required express authorisation for any banking.
　　- Wisconsin debated deposit powers until 1909, when "trust-company banks" were created with power to receive time and savings deposits but not deposits subject to check.
　　- By the time of writing, most States defined a trust company as "a bank which has power to act in the capacity of trustee, administrator, guardian, or executor."

### 限制与边界

　　- The source does not provide aggregate data on the scale of trust-company banking relative to trust business.
　　- The evolution is described as primarily economic rather than legislative, but the examples are selective and do not establish a representative national pattern.

### 原文摘录

> The development of the trust company as reflected in the legislation with reference to its powers shows two main tendencies: (1) The companies have to a very large extent given up the insuring of the fidelity of persons in positions of trust and the guaranteeing of land titles. (2) They have largely increased their banking activities.
> The result of the two tendencies described above... has gradually standardized the powers of the trust company, until at the present time the trust company, as it appears in the corporation laws of most of the States, may be fairly well defined as a bank which has power to act in the capacity of trustee, administrator, guardian, or executor.
