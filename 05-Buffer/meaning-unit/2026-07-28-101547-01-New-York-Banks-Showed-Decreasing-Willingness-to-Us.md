### 核心表达

Comparison of the crises of 1873, 1893, and 1907 shows that New York banks became progressively less willing to draw down their cash reserves. In 1873 they exhausted reserves almost to the minimum and recovered quickly; in 1893 and 1907 they restricted payments while still holding very large reserves and then hoarded additional cash, prolonging suspension.

### 依据与细节

**1873:**
- Available reserve at the outset: **$34,300,000**.
- After four weeks: reduced to **$5,800,000**, a ratio to deposit liabilities of **less than 4.5 per cent**.
- Suspension occurred but was shorter than in later crises.
- Recovery began within three weeks after the outbreak.
- Special difficulties: government had no surplus greenbacks, banknote circulation was legally capped, and imported gold was unusable for ordinary banking because business was conducted in inconvertible paper currency.

**1893:**
- Early June cash reserve exceeded **30 per cent of net deposits**.
- Suspended in the first week of August while still holding **$79,000,000**, more than **20 per cent of deposit liabilities**.
- Favorable factors were available: large gold imports arranged by end of July, heavy foreign purchases of U.S. securities reflecting confidence in silver-purchase-law repeal, and expected increases in bank-note issues for August and September.

**1907:**
- Restricted payments while holding more than **$220,000,000** in cash reserve, also above **20 per cent**.
- Unlike 1873, after restriction the banks hoarded reserves and added to them, prolonging suspension.

### 限制与边界

The comparison mixes different monetary regimes: 1873 operated under inconvertible paper currency, while later crises had more elastic cash sources. The author treats the 1873 depletion strategy as superior, but the special inability to obtain cash in 1873 may have forced that behaviour rather than revealing a different institutional culture. Reserve figures are aggregates and do not show distribution among individual banks.

### 原文摘录

> "In 1873 the New York banks at the outset of the crisis held an available reserve of $34,300,000. In the course of four weeks this was reduced to $5,800,000, and the ratio to deposit liabilities was then less than 4.5 per cent."

> "Notwithstanding all these favorable circumstances the New York banks suspended, during the first week of August, when they still held a cash reserve of $79,000,000, more than 20 per cent. of their deposit liabilities."

> "Both in 1893 and in 1907 suspension was not a measure of last resort taken after the banks had entirely exhausted their reserves and when there was no means of securing additional cash resources."
