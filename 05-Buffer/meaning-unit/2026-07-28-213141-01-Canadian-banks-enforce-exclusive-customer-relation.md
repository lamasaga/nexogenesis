### 核心表达

　　Canadian commercial banking is built on intimate, exclusive bank-customer relationships: a customer deals with one bank, discloses his affairs fully each year, and must obtain consent before raising funds elsewhere.

### 依据与细节

　　- A business man deals exclusively with one bank; once a year he arranges a line of credit and learns exactly how much paper he can discount.
　　- Interest is paid only on the portion of the credit actually utilized.
　　- The bank may increase the line if business conditions and prospects warrant expansion.
　　- The customer must not seek funds elsewhere without the bank's consent; selling notes in the open market without the bank's knowledge risks being placed on the "black list."
　　- As a result, little commercial paper floats in the Canadian money market; the bill broker is unknown, and wholesalers/manufacturers do not normally draw upon domestic customers.

### 原文摘录

> Among Canadian bankers, therefore, it is part of the law and gospel of banking that a bank is entitled to full knowledge of the financial condition and business operations and prospects of its customers.
> Once a year he arranges with his bank for a line of credit and learns exactly the amount of paper he will be able to discount.
> Under no circumstances, however, must the customer of a bank seek to raise funds elsewhere unless he first gets the consent of his bank.
> As one would naturally expect, there is very little commercial paper floating about in the Canadian money market. The bill broker is unknown.
