### 核心表达

The ratio of end-of-day balances to total clearings depends partly on the number of banks in a clearing house, but much more on the amount and character of their business and on their relations with one another. The more equal the participating banks, the more completely their transactions offset and the lower the balance ratio tends to be.

### 依据与细节

Figures collected for the year 1908 show wide variation:

- **Pittsburg**: 20 members, 128 non-members clearing through members, balances 16.5 per cent of clearings.
- **Buffalo**: 11 members, 7 non-members, balances 12 per cent of clearings.
- **Chicago**: 20 members, 40 non-members clearing through members, balances 7.5 per cent of clearings.
- **Philadelphia**: 31 members, 1 non-member, balances 11.5 per cent of clearings.
- **St. Louis**: 17 members, 35 non-members, balances 9.3 per cent of clearings.
- **New York**: average over fifty-four years of existence, balances only 4.64 per cent of clearings, despite the persistent heavy debit balance of the United States assistant treasurer.

The author infers that near-equality among banks produces nearly complete offset, leaving little balance to settle.

### 限制与边界

The data are single-year (1908) except for New York's long-run average. No predictive formula is offered. Regional settlement-method differences may also influence the ratio, but are not controlled for. The New York figure includes a government account that is structurally different from ordinary member business.

### 原文摘录

> "The ratio of balances to clearings depends partly upon the number of banks, but much more upon the amount and character of their business and upon their relations one to another."

> "At Pittsburg, with 20 members and 128 non-members clearing through members, the balances were 16.5 per cent. of the clearings; at Buffalo, with 11 members and 7 non-members, 12 per cent.; at Chicago, with 20 members and 40 non-members clearing through members, 7.5 per cent."

> "The more nearly the banks stand on an equality with one another, the more nearly will their transactions approach a complete offset, which, of course, would leave no balance to settle."
