### 核心表达

　　For American importers, Dollar Credits were more economical than sterling or continental credits because they carried lower initial commission costs, eliminated exchange risk, and benefited from lower New York discount rates. Beyond individual savings, the generalized use of Dollar Credits was presented as a national interest that could reduce the annual tribute paid to foreign money centers and raise the international prestige of the dollar.

### 依据与细节

　　**Direct economies:**
- New York discount rate for prime bank acceptances: **2-1/8@2-1/4 per cent per annum**;
- London discount rate for prime ninety-day bills: **4-3/4 per cent per annum**;
- Interest-rate advantage in favor of New York: roughly **2-1/2@2-5/8 per cent per annum**;
- Commission-cost advantage for New York dollar credits over London sterling credits: about **1/2 per cent per annum**;
- Combined net saving: **3@3-1/8 per cent per annum**.

　　**Risk elimination:** Dollar Credits are based on the dollar, a "known quantity," so maturities are due and payable in dollars on a given date without concern for the London exchange rate ninety days after acceptance.

　　**National scale and strategic argument:**
- United States imports grew from **$1,025,719,237 in 1903** to **$1,893,925,657 in 1914**;
- Despite high international credit standing, the United States had relied on "indirect channels" and foreign money centers to finance imports, costing "millions of dollars annually in interest, commissions, etc.";
- American branch banks in South America created a direct liquidation channel, especially with Argentina, Brazil, and Uruguay, removing the former disparity against the dollar versus sterling and continental exchanges;
- The author calls co-operation by American merchants to generalize Dollar Credits a "duty" that would bring "lasting benefit to the economic fabric of our commerce."

### 限制与边界

　　原文未提及： the proportion of imports actually shifted to Dollar Credits by 1915; whether the savings were sustainable after the European war ended and London markets normalized; the counterargument that London's deeper market might still offer better liquidity for non-standard tenors or countries.

### 原文摘录

> "Primarily, it is more economical than the Sterling or Continental Credit, for the initial commission cost of issuance is lower."
> "A comparison of these two discount rates will show a difference in favor of New York of 2-1/2@2-5/8 per cent. per annum."
> "Co-operation and concerted action on the part of our merchants to the end of generalizing the use of Dollar Credits is therefore a duty, which will bring about lasting benefit to the economic fabric of our commerce."
