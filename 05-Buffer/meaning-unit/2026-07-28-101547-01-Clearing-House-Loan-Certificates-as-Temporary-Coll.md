### 核心表达

Clearing-house loan certificates are temporary, collateral-secured loans made by the associated banks of a clearing house to their members for the sole purpose of settling clearing-house balances during financial disturbances. They expand the effective volume of currency available to the banking system without entering general circulation or being seen by the business community.

### 依据与细节

Two instruments are distinguished:

1. **Certificates on deposit of gold coin** (in New York and Boston also gold/silver certificates and legal-tender notes): used in ordinary times to economize handling risk.
2. **Loan certificates on collateral securities**: used in panics; these are the focus of the discussion.

Operational mechanics observed in the text:

- A **loan committee** of usually five bank officers, plus the clearing-house president ex officio, meets each morning at the clearing house.
- The committee examines collateral and issues certificates in agreed denominations and proportions.
- Historical denominations ranged from **25 cents to $100,000**.
- Issue ratios varied from **$50 to $100 of certificates per $100 of collateral deposited**.
- Interest rates ranged from **5 to 10 per cent per annum**, paid by debtor banks to creditor banks receiving certificates in settlement.
- Certificates are retired as soon as the issuing banks obtain sufficient cash; interest ceases on a specified date; collateral is surrendered proportionally.

### 限制与边界

Certificates are negotiable only among clearing-house members, are not currency, and do not pass from bank to bank except in payment of clearing-house balances. Uptake is uneven: strong banks may refuse them to avoid signalling weakness, while weak banks may be driven to them by necessity. The text notes that some bankers viewed their use as "a patriotic movement," others as "a reflection upon their standing."

### 原文摘录

> "Clearing-house loan certificates may be defined as temporary loans made by the banks associated together as a clearing-house association, to the members thereof, for the purpose of settling clearing-house balances."

> "In the past the denominations have varied from 25 cents to $100,000 in the different associations and in proportions varying from $50 to $100 of certificates to $100 of collateral deposited."

> "These loan certificates bear interest at rates varying from 5 to 10 per cent. per annum, payable by the banks to which they are issued to the banks receiving such certificates in settlement of daily balances."
