### 核心表达

　　The 1913 revision of the Canadian Bank Act introduced three major changes: a shareholders' audit, central gold reserves to back note issues, and expanded authority for banks to lend to farmers on stored grain.

### 依据与细节

　　- The revision was delayed from 1910 to 1913. Public concern over bank inspection intensified after the failure of the Farmers' Bank, which had gambled its resources on the Keeley mine.
　　- Shareholders' audit: auditors are chosen by shareholders from a list of forty names selected by the general managers and approved by the Minister of Finance; one-third of shareholders may appeal for another auditor.
　　- Central gold reserves: banks may issue notes to any desired amount if they deposit gold or Dominion notes with trustees at Montreal; notes are identical to ordinary notes. Banks may still issue notes up to paid-up capital plus legal-tender deposits without central backing, and an additional 15 per cent of capital and surplus from September 1 to end of February. A 4 per cent tax applies to the extra crop-season issue.
　　- Farm grain loans: banks may now lend on grain stored on the farm and still in the farmer's possession, extending to farmers the kind of accommodation previously given to manufacturers and wholesalers.

### 原文摘录

> The essential changes effected were: (1) provision for a shareholders' audit, (2) the creation of central gold reserves, and (3) the providing of additional facilities for making loans to farmers.
> Under the new act each bank may issue any amount of notes that it may desire, provided that it deposits with a board of trustees, at Montreal, gold or Dominion notes to the full amount of the notes issued.
> Many bankers, however, refuse to see any remedy for the situation in the new legislation. They maintain that it will involve too much risk to extend loans on grain over which the farmer continues to assert control.
