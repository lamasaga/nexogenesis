### 核心表达

　　The London Bankers' Clearing House evolved from an exclusive private-bank club into a three-tier national cheque-clearing system (town, metropolitan, country) by absorbing joint-stock banks, country bankers, and eventually the Bank of England, while remaining a voluntary private association rather than a public regulator.

### 依据与细节

　　- Joint-stock banks were admitted to the clearing house but initially only rented seats and had no management share; they maintained a separate committee to settle the rate for deposit money at seven days' notice in the London district.
　　- In 1858 country bankers proposed a separate country clearing house in London; John Lubbock (later Lord Avebury) instead designed a country clearing operated within the existing Bankers' Clearing House, approved by both the clearing-house committee and the country bankers' committee.
　　- The country clearing "more than all else" brought about the almost universal use of cheques in England, displacing notes and coin.
　　- In 1864 the Bank of England entered the clearing house to clear only on the outside: it presented to clearing bankers all cheques payable by them, while cheques and bills drawn on the Bank of England were presented by clearing bankers at the Bank itself, with proceeds credited to each bank's account. The Governor of the Bank of England became an ex officio member of the committee of clearing bankers.
　　- In 1907 a third clearing, the Metropolitan, was established to collect branch cheques through the clearing house rather than by post and walk clerks; over 330 banks and branches used it.
　　- Letters T, M, and C were placed in the corner of all bank cheques to identify the three clearing areas.
　　- From 19 February 1907 (initiation of the Metropolitan Clearing) to 31 December 1907, £482,227,000 was paid in the Metropolitan clearing; for 1908 the total was £647,842,000, compared with town clearing £10,408,254,000 and country clearing £1,064,266,000, making a grand total of £12,120,362,000 (a decrease of £610,031,000 from the 1907 total of £12,730,393,000).
　　- Adding machines were installed because the volume could "scarcely have been dealt with by hand alone."

### 限制与边界

　　- The text focuses on England and gives only brief mention of eight provincial clearing houses (Birmingham, Bristol, Leeds, Leicester, Liverpool, Manchester, Newcastle, Sheffield).
　　- The source states that not all cheques on London went through the clearing house: cheques on London branches of Scotch, colonial, and foreign banks were still presented over the counter.
　　- The mutual agreement among clearing banks to present cheques on each other only through the clearing house had no legal binding; exceptions were continually made when documents or goods had to be taken up against cash.

### 原文摘录

> Thus was instituted at the Bankers' Clearing House the country clearing, which more than all else has brought about the almost universal use of checks in England, to the exclusion of notes and coin.
> In 1864 the Bank of England entered the clearing house to clear on one side only, the outside, for though the bank presents to the clearing bankers at the clearing house all checks payable by them, all checks and bills drawn on the bank are presented by the clearing bankers at the bank itself, and the proceeds placed to the credit of each bank's account.
> It must not be thought that all checks on London are presented through the clearing house, for checks on the London branches of the Scotch banks and of the colonial and foreign banks are still presented over the counter.
