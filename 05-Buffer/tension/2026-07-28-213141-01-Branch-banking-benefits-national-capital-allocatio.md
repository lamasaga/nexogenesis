### 核心表达

　　There is a tension between the national benefits of branch banking in equalizing capital supply and its possible local costs in retarding industrial development in the slower-growing maritime provinces.

### 对立双方

　　- **National-efficiency view**: Branch banking moves savings from capital-surplus Eastern provinces to capital-hungry Western territories, equalizing interest rates and financing national development.
　　- **Regional-development view**: The same transfer drains local capital from the East; had independent local banks existed, the East would have had a plethora of capital and could have developed industries comparable to those of Massachusetts.

### 核心分歧点

　　- Whether the observed low interest-rate differentials and Western growth are worth the foregone Eastern industrialization.
　　- Whether a counterfactual system of independent local banks, like that of the United States, would have produced lower rates (4–5 per cent) in Montreal, Quebec, and Halifax and higher rates (1 per cent per month) in smaller Western cities.

### 限制与边界

　　原文未提及：该论断基于反事实推理，未提供直接证据；未量化“被抑制”的东部产业数量或就业损失；未讨论美国东北部工业繁荣是否主要由低资本成本驱动而非其他因素。

### 原文摘录

> Justifiable as the bank's policy is from a national point of view, one can not help believing that the branch banking system has really checked the development of business and industry in the maritime Provinces.
> If Canada during the last thirty years had depended, like the United States, upon independent local banks, there would have been a plethora of capital in the East, and Montreal, Quebec, and Halifax, like Boston, New York, and Philadelphia, would years ago have had 4 and 5 per cent. money, while Winnipeg and other Western cities, less populous than now, would still be paying 1 per cent. a month.
> The relative cheapness of capital undoubtedly helped build up the prosperous industries of Massachusetts. The same cause operating in the maritime Provinces of Canada would doubtless have led to the establishment there of industries of which the people under existing conditions have not ventured to dream.
