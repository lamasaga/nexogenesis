### 核心表达

　　There is a fundamental regulatory tension between the original view that trust companies are institutions of markedly different character from ordinary banks and the emerging view that they are merely banks exercising additional functions, with implications for whether their banking activities should be assimilated to state-bank regulation.

### 对立双方

　　- **Traditional / fiduciary view**: Trust companies were corporations specialised in administering trusts, with incidental insurance and safe-deposit powers; they required regulation distinct from that of banks.
　　- **Functional / banking view**: As trust companies increased the scope and amount of their banking business, their banking regulation should be assimilated to the regulations imposed on state banks.

### 核心分歧点

　　- Whether the corporate purpose (trust administration) or the economic function (receiving deposits, making loans) should determine the regulatory regime.
　　- Whether judicial interpretation and business-method adaptation should be allowed to expand banking powers beyond what legislators explicitly granted.
　　- Whether states should maintain separate capital and supervisory rules for trust companies or treat them as a variant of state banks.

### 限制与边界

　　- The source describes convergence rather than an active political dispute; the "tension" is structural and jurisprudential, not necessarily a recorded debate.
　　- No empirical evidence is given on whether assimilation improved depositor safety or systemic stability.

### 原文摘录

> The gradual change from the view that the trust company is an institution of markedly different character from the ordinary bank of discount and deposit to the view that the trust company is merely a bank exercising functions additional to those exercised by the majority of banks has been the chief influence in determining the form of the legal regulations imposed upon trust companies.
> As long as the older view obtained, the regulations concerning trust companies were widely different from those imposed upon banks; but as the trust company has increased both the scope and amount of its banking business, the regulation of the banking business of the trust company has tended to become assimilated to the regulations imposed upon state banks.
