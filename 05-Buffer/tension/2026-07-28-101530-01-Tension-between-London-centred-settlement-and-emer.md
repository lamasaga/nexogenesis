### 核心表达

　　The established international payments architecture centered on London sterling credits was being challenged by the post-1913 rise of an American acceptance market and Dollar Credits, which promised lower cost, exchange-risk elimination for importers, and a direct channel for United States–South American liquidation.

### 对立双方

- **London / sterling-credit camp:** Prior to the war, roughly 95 per cent of United States import credits were sterling credits on London. London's discount market was deep, its currency stable, and its bills universally salable. British traders sold in sterling and were relatively indifferent to exchange-rate movements.
- **New York / dollar-credit camp:** The Federal Reserve Act granted national banks acceptance privileges; American branch banks in South America opened direct liquidation channels; Dollar Credits eliminated exchange risk for the importer and, by 1915, offered a combined interest-and-commission saving estimated at 3 to 3-1/8 per cent per annum.

### 核心分歧点

- **Cost:** New York prime ninety-day bank acceptances discounted at 2-1/8@2-1/4 per cent per annum, while London prime ninety-day bills discounted at 4-3/4 per cent per annum—a difference of roughly 2-1/2 per cent before commissions. With commission savings of about 1/2 per cent, Dollar Credits yielded a net saving of 3@3-1/8 per cent per annum.
- **Exchange risk:** Dollar Credits are based on a "known quantity, the dollar," so the importer knows the maturity amount in dollars and does not face uncertainty about the London exchange rate ninety days after acceptance.
- **Market maturity:** The American acceptance market was described as "still in its infancy" in 1915, whereas the London market was long-established and broad.
- **Prestige and network effects:** The author argues it was "unquestionably opportune" to increase the prestige of the dollar and standardize its use, implying a strategic national benefit beyond individual transaction savings.

### 限制与边界

　　原文未提及： whether London responded by cutting rates or commissions; the actual volume shift from sterling to dollar credits after 1914; legal or regulatory obstacles to dollar-credit adoption outside South America; the persistence of these savings in "normal" peacetime conditions.

### 原文摘录

> "Prior to the outbreak of the war in Europe, it is safe to assume that fully 95 per cent. of the credits issued to cover these importations were passed through London in the form of sterling credits."
> "A comparison of these two discount rates will show a difference in favor of New York of 2-1/2@2-5/8 per cent. per annum."
