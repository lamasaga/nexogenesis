### 核心表达

　　There is a fundamental disagreement between the apparent American expectation that bank notes need legal-tender status or government backing and the Canadian bankers' view that such attributes would weaken the note by encouraging the public to hoard it as money.

### 对立双方

- **American / external observers**: The lack of legal-tender quality and government guaranty looks like weakness; in the United States, national-bank notes are secured by bond deposits and government involvement is taken for granted.
- **Canadian bankers**: Making bank notes legal tender or government-guaranteed would be harmful, like "hampering a flying machine with unnecessary bars of steel," because it would encourage ignorant classes to treat notes as money itself and withdraw them from circulation.

### 核心分歧点

　　Whether the security of a bank note is better provided by market discipline (daily redemption, clearing houses, first lien, double liability) or by sovereign compulsion (legal tender, government guarantee). The Canadian position treats the note as a promise to pay that should return to the issuer when its work is done; the American framework treats note issue as a privilege requiring collateral and public enforcement.

### 限制与边界

- The text presents the Canadian bankers' view as unanimous ("would doubtless all agree") without reporting dissent.
- The characterization of the ignorant classes is the author's framing, not a measured behavioral claim.
- 原文未提及：empirical evidence on whether legal-tender notes are actually hoarded more than non-legal-tender notes.

### 原文摘录

> "The two negative qualities of the Canadian bank note--its lack of a legal-tender quality and of a government guaranty--at first sight may seem to readers in the United States a source of weakness."
> "It would be like hampering a flying machine with unnecessary bars of steel."
> "If it is made a legal tender like money itself, or if its redemption is guaranteed by a strong government, there is always the danger that ignorant classes of people will regard it as money itself and withdraw it from circulation."
