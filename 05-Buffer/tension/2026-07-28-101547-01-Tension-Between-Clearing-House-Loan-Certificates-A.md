### 核心表达

The critical question is whether issuing clearing-house loan certificates by themselves can prevent suspension, or whether they must be accompanied by an equalization of member-bank reserves. The 1873 New York arrangement combined both devices, while 1884, 1893, and 1907 relied on loan certificates alone. The author argues that the abandonment of reserve equalization was the decisive reason suspension recurred in 1893 and 1907.

### 对立双方

**Side A: Loan certificates alone are sufficient.**
- Proponents point to 1884 and 1890, when New York weathered moderate storms without suspension by using loan certificates alone.
- Some strong banks regarded reserve pooling as an unfair subsidy to weaker competitors and resisted it after 1873.
- In 1884 opposition to equalization was so widespread that it was not even formally considered.

**Side B: Loan certificates alone invite suspension unless reserves are equalized.**
- Once all banks settle balances in certificates, banks receiving drafts on other banks cannot obtain cash from those banks; partial suspension becomes inevitable.
- Without pooling, individual banks can try to strengthen themselves at the expense of others, undermining the common purpose.
- In 1893 and 1907 suspension occurred while New York banks still held large cash reserves (more than $79,000,000 and more than $220,000,000 respectively), because the banks hoarded reserves rather than using them.

### 核心分歧点

The disagreement centers on whether the problem is a lack of cash or a lack of coordination. Side A treats each bank's reserve as its own responsibility and sees loan certificates as a temporary substitute for cash. Side B treats reserves as a collective resource during a panic and sees equalization as the mechanism that prevents any single bank from defensive hoarding that forces suspension on all.

### 限制与边界

The argument is partly counterfactual: the author claims that 1873-style reserve equalization would probably have prevented suspension in 1893 and 1907, but this cannot be proven. The text itself was written before the 1913 banking reform, so it does not assess how the Federal Reserve changed the underlying mechanics. Objections to pooling after 1873 were based on perceived evasions and unfairness, which the author dismisses as illusory but which were politically real.

### 原文摘录

> "The connection between suspension and the use of clearing-house loan certificates as the sole medium of payment between the banks is simple and direct."

> "That the arrangement for equalizing the reserves, adopted in 1873, would have availed to prevent suspension on subsequent occasions, is highly probable, indeed a practical certainty."

> "The contention of bankers both in 1893 and in 1907 that they had not suspended since they had only refused to honor drafts on other banks was untenable."
