### 核心表达

　　The Canadian banking system may be understood not as a financial novelty but as an evolutionary continuation of several pre-Civil War American banking institutions—the New York **safety-fund system**, the **State Bank of Indiana**'s branch banking, the **Suffolk redemption system**, and the free-banking bond-deposit experiment—operating in a Canadian political and commercial context that was not disrupted by the Civil War.

### 依据与细节

- The safety-fund system introduced in New York in 1829 was adopted in Canada and remains an integral part of the Canadian banking system.
- Branch banking, illustrated in the United States by the State Bank of Indiana, has always prevailed in Canada.
- Prompt redemption of bank notes, exemplified by the Suffolk banking system in New England before the Civil War, is realized in Canada; notes and checks are cleared with the same regularity and promptness.
- The free-banking system adopted in New York in 1839 and later by eighteen other states was tried in Canada in the 1850s but on a small scale and never won approval among leading Canadian bankers.
- The author speculates that "but for the Civil War what is now known as the Canadian banking system would everywhere be called the American system."

### 限制与边界

- This is a historical interpretive claim rather than a formal institutional genealogy; specific statutes and dates of adoption in Canada are not provided.
- The Civil War is treated as the decisive divergence point for American banking, but the text does not trace exact post-war American reforms.
- 原文未提及：direct archival or legislative evidence linking Canadian statutes to specific American precedents.
