### 可能关联

The 1873 New York arrangement that pooled legal-tender reserves and empowered a committee to equalize them may be an early private-sector prototype of the centralized reserve pooling later assigned to a public central bank. The failure of loan certificates alone in 1893 and 1907 suggests that a lender-of-last-resort function requires both emergency liquidity and coordinated reserve management, not merely collateralized settlement credit.

### 触发点

- The text explicitly notes it was written "before our banking reform of 1913," implying that the defects it diagnoses were addressed by the Federal Reserve Act.
- The 1873 resolution treated member banks' legal tenders as a "common fund, held for mutual aid and protection" and authorized a committee to equalize reserves by assessment.
- The author calls the absence of equalization after 1873 "the most serious element of weakness in our credit structure."

### 待验证

Whether the 1913 Act's regional reserve banks and rediscounting authority were designed precisely to replace the clearing-house loan-certificate mechanism with a permanent, statutory reserve pool; and whether contemporary observers of the Act cited the 1873 equalization experience as a precedent.

> "That in order to accomplish the purposes set forth in this agreement the legal tenders belonging to the associated banks shall be considered and treated as a common fund, held for mutual aid and protection, and the committee appointed shall have power to equalize the same by assessment or otherwise at their discretion."
